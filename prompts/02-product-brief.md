## WHAT TO PRODUCE

The landing page, advertorial and creatives may be in any language. Read them
in whatever language they are in. Write your entire output in English — except
the verbatim buyer phrases in section 8, which stay in their original language
with an English gloss in brackets.

Markdown, these sections, no preamble.

### 1. The product
What it is, what it actually does, and the mechanism by which it does it. Then
the claims you can defend — drawn strictly from the pages supplied. Note the price structure and offer as stated, including variants. If the LP makes a claim nothing substantiates, list it separately
under "unsupported claims currently live". Do not invent claims. An invented
claim produces refunds and chargebacks, which kill a dropshipping store faster
than a bad creative ever will.

### 2. The buyer
Who buys this, in plain language. Their situation, not a demographic table.

### 3. Awareness
Which stage the traffic sits in, and why. State it as a judgement with reasoning,
not a percentage breakdown you cannot support.

### 4. Market sophistication (1–5)
This section matters more than the last one and is usually skipped.

- Stage 1: nobody is making this claim yet — the plain claim works
- Stage 2: the claim is being made — you must out-claim
- Stage 3: claims are exhausted — you compete on **mechanism**
- Stage 4: your mechanism is being copied — you need a *better* mechanism
- Stage 5: mechanisms are exhausted — you compete on **identification**

Health & beauty is almost always 3–5. Name the stage and what it demands. A
stage-1 claim in a stage-4 market is the single most common reason a
well-produced creative dies.

### 5. What the winners share
From the teardown. The common thread across winners — angle, desire, emotion,
proof type. Be specific about what is actually shared versus what is
coincidence across a small sample.

### 6. What the losers got wrong
Per loser, what the metric shape says to change. Separate the ones where the
*visual* failed from the ones where the *promise* failed — they call for
opposite fixes in the next batch, and conflating them is how a batch of fifteen
gets aimed at the wrong problem.

### 7. Untested territory
The most important section. From the research and the problem dossier, list:

- **Desires** not yet targeted by any creative
- **Beliefs** — things the buyer currently believes that block the sale, and
  things they must come to believe, which no creative has yet addressed
- **Buyers** — segments the existing creatives do not speak to
- **Failed solutions** — what this buyer has already tried and been burned by.
  This is the richest angle source in health & beauty and it is nearly always
  under-used. What they blame themselves for failing at is what you can
  absolve them of.
- **Myths** worth correcting, and the hidden cause behind the problem

Each item: one line, plus one line on why it is plausibly live. Do not pad this
list to look thorough. Six real openings beat twenty invented ones.

### 8. Voice
A dozen verbatim phrases the buyer actually uses, from reviews and the dossier.
Then a short list of words to keep out — clinical, marketing, or writerly
language this buyer would never use.
