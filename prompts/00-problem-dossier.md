## PRODUCE

Markdown, no preamble.

### The problem itself
Causes, mechanism, how it progresses, who it affects and when it typically
starts. Plain language.

### The lived experience
What it is actually like day to day. What is embarrassing about it. What it
stops people doing. What they hide, and from whom. This section is where new
angles come from, so spend the most effort here — the clinical description
above generates nothing.

### Failed solutions
What sufferers have already tried and why each failed them. For each, note what
they concluded from the failure — usually something self-blaming. This is the
single richest angle source available: the ad that absolves someone of a failure
they blame themselves for gets read to the end.

### Vocabulary
How real sufferers describe it in their own words. Verbatim phrases wherever
possible. Then the clinical or marketing terms they never use.

### Myths and misconceptions
Widely-held beliefs about this problem that are wrong, and the hidden cause
people miss. Each of these is a potential myth-bust ad.

### Triggers and timing
Seasonality, life events, age thresholds, situations that make the problem
suddenly salient.

### Buyer segments
The distinct groups who have this problem for different reasons or experience it
differently. Different segments need different ads even for the same product.

## RULES

Ground everything you can. Where you are inferring rather than reporting, mark
the line `[INFERRED]` so the next reader knows which parts to verify. A dossier
that quietly mixes evidence with invention gets trusted and then produces
fifteen ads built on something nobody actually feels.
